## Why three tasks?

<SectionLead>

Limiting yourself to three tasks per day creates focus and makes progress visible. Here’s how the blocks below fit together.

</SectionLead>

<KeyIdea>

The rule of three is simple: each day, choose exactly three tasks that would make the day a success. Everything else is secondary.

</KeyIdea>

<Highlight>

Choose your three tasks the night before or first thing in the morning—and don’t add a fourth until the first three are done.

</Highlight>

<Tip>

Write your three tasks the night before or first thing in the morning. Keep them visible so you stay focused.

</Tip>

<Example>

**Morning:** Review your three tasks over coffee. **Afternoon:** Block 90 minutes to work through them without switching context.

</Example>

<PullQuote author="Greg McKeown" source="Essentialism">

If it isn’t a clear yes, then it’s a clear no.

</PullQuote>

<Checklist title="Daily Focus Checklist">
<ul>
<li>Pick three tasks that matter most today</li>
<li>Do the hardest one first</li>
<li>Resist adding a fourth until the first three are done</li>
</ul>
</Checklist>
